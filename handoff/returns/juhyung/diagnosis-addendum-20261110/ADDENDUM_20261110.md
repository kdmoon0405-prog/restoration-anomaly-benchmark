# Addendum to REPORT.md (2026-11-10): FPR plateaus, affine normalization, peak-in-GT, spatial cases

This addendum leaves `REPORT.md` (commit `fcab7ef`) unchanged. It corrects the interpretation using the CPU review `docs/GPU_FOLLOWUP_REVIEW_20261110.md` and `analysis/gpu_followup_20261110/empirical_fpr_plateaus.csv` on `handoff/juhyung`, plus the 9-case spatial analysis run on 2026-11-10. Frozen q0.99 thresholds and all original results stay as they were.

## A. Matched-FPR values were plateau endpoints (correction)

`pcb_matched_fpr_tpr.csv` placed the threshold just above the (k+1)-th highest test normal. That gives the **highest** TPR among all thresholds producing the same k false positives. Any threshold between the k-th and (k+1)-th normal gives the same empirical FPR, so TPR is a range:

| test FP / 100 normals | clean TPR | Bicubic TPR | SwinIR TPR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.43–0.65 | 0.55–0.57 | 0.39–0.57 |
| 3 | 0.68–0.71 | 0.62–0.82 | 0.59–0.70 |
| 5 | 0.82–0.84 | 0.84–0.86 | 0.71–0.72 |
| 10 | 0.97 | 0.94–0.95 | 0.88 |

Consequences for REPORT.md §2:
- "At an equal 1% test FPR, SwinIR and Bicubic have the same TPR (0.57)" holds only at the optimistic endpoints. The ranges are 0.55–0.57 for Bicubic and 0.39–0.57 for SwinIR.
- "If SwinIR's threshold had landed at 3 FP, its TPR would be 0.70 (F1 ≈ 0.81)" is the upper end. The range is 59–70 TP, F1 ≈ 0.73–0.81.
- **Withdrawn:** "Operating point, the larger part". One empirical-FPR point cannot attribute most of the F1 drop to the threshold transfer. Both the realized operating point (1 vs 3 test FP) and a ranking difference contribute, and with 100 test normals they cannot be separated reliably.

## B. Positive affine normalization preserves rank and the q0.99 decision (clarification)

The image normalization `(x − median) / scale` with scale > 0 is a positive affine map fitted on calibration normals:
- It preserves the order of scores, so AUROC is unchanged.
- Thresholding at the normalized q0.99 is the same decision as thresholding the raw score at the raw-calibration q0.99.

This was checked in the reconstruction: `q0.99_raw_then_normalize` equals the frozen threshold in `pcb_threshold_sensitivity_diagnostic.csv`.

So REPORT.md §2.2's "the robust scale is larger, so normalized anomaly scores are smaller" describes units, not a mechanism. The explanation must be stated in raw-score terms:
- SwinIR raw calibration/test-normal scores have a wider spread: IQR 0.481 vs 0.400 on all 280 normals, 0.513 vs 0.385 on the 180 calibration normals.
- Anomaly raw medians are similar: 6.65 vs 6.71.

The normalization operation itself is not a cause.

## C. Peak-in-GT is an auxiliary observation, not a localization assessment (clarification)

- REPORT.md §2.3 called the result "Not a localization failure (confirmed on saved maps)". That overstates it. On the 25 Bicubic-TP→SwinIR-FN boards, the overlap-mean map argmax lies inside GT on 68% (Bicubic 56%). This says where a single maximum lies. It does not evaluate localization over the board.
- A peak inside GT can coexist with a per-image AU-PRO regression. The 9-case spatial analysis below shows exactly this: suppression boards keep high-valued ROI, while the ROI–background gap shrinks.
- The "argmax tile" is a proxy taken from the overlap-mean map. It is not the argmax of the actual tile image scores. Actual tile scores were recomputed for only 1 board × 2 variants.
- The same caution applies to Screw §3: "the peak leaves the defect" describes the maximum only. Pixel-level localization is reported separately (AU-PRO 0.80–0.86).

## D. Matched-bank control: what recovery and non-recovery would mean (correction)

- REPORT.md §3 said a matched bank would "not [restore AUROC] if the ×4 LR step destroys the defect evidence". That is too strong.
- Recovery under a matched-domain bank, with queries fixed and only the normal reference changed, would support a reference-domain effect.
- Non-recovery alone would not establish information loss. Backbone representation, bank support (capacity and coverage) and the image-score aggregation (max) remain alternative explanations.

## E. 9-case native-map spatial analysis (exploratory; selected cases, not a population estimate)

- **Run:**
  - `scripts/analyze_pcb_selected_maps.py` from `handoff/juhyung@1e6c6bc` (delivery worktree)
  - inputs: `--maps-root outputs/pcb-full-cohort/pcb-full-cohort-20261101-a`, `--metadata-root handoff/metadata` (8 files, hashes verified), `--data-root data/external/VisA`
  - output: `outputs/native-spatial-20261110-152518` (local)
- **Result:** COMPLETE, 9 boards / 27 maps, 4-connectivity, native GT verified, 27 raw maps byte-verified, originals not rewritten.
- **Memory guard:** the fixed guard passed on this host. The payload plan was 0.42 GB per board plus the 2 GiB reserve. It is a plan, not a measured peak.
- **Runtime:** 27 s wall.
- **Figures:** the 9 PNGs contain RGB and GT, so they stay local. Three were opened and checked: one suppression, one geometry and one success case. Each sample uses one shared normalized colour scale, with tile boundaries and GT components shown.

Observations (descriptive, n = 9 selected boards):
- **Tile seams** (fixed `tile_boxes` boundaries):
  - In every board and variant, the mean |adjacent difference| across seams (0.44–0.79) is about 13–25× the non-seam mean (0.026–0.036).
  - The seam transition is a property of overlap-mean assembly and is present in clean too. It is larger after ×4 round trips in 9/9 boards (clean 0.44–0.60, Bicubic 0.57–0.74, SwinIR 0.63–0.79).
  - GT pixels on seam lines: 0.7–8.8%.
  - This is a descriptive statistic, not a cause or a performance metric.
- **ROI–background gap (normalized):**
  - Clean is largest in 9/9.
  - SwinIR−Bicubic deltas: −0.10 / −0.24 / −0.17 for suppression, +0.09 / +1.06 / +0.02 for geometry_candidate, +0.39 / +0.57 / +0.58 for success.
  - The cases were selected on delta AU-PRO, so this sign pattern is expected by construction and is not independent evidence.
- **Spatial agreement:** Pearson correlation between the SwinIR and Bicubic maps is 0.89–0.98. Positive-excess centroid displacement is 0.9–17 px on 1404×1070 boards. This is not a defect-position error.
- **Visual check:**
  - 047 (suppression): two tiny GT components inside a brightly responding component area. After ×4, ROI and background both drop, with no visible spatial shift.
  - 006 (geometry_candidate): the bent pins stand out in all three variants. SwinIR adds dark bands along chip edges that are not present in Bicubic.
  - 050 (success_control): weak GT-pin response in all variants, with peaks on other components.
